# EquityTeam Brand Voice & Positioning

The canonical source of truth for what the EquityTeam website must communicate, who it speaks to, and the tone it uses. Pair with the `equityteam-typography` skill for visual styling decisions.

## Purpose Statement (EOS / Vision Statement)

> **Helping People & Properties Prosper**

This is the canonical purpose/vision statement. It belongs on the homepage hero as the gold eyebrow above the H1 — the first line a visitor reads, framing everything that follows. Use it verbatim; do not paraphrase or shorten. It applies to all audiences: owners, residents, investors, and homeowners.

## One-line positioning

EquityTeam is **far and away the best, most experienced, highest-rated, win/win, and ethical property management company** for an owner to trust their asset with — across every service line.

## Service lines

| Line | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term rental residential property management | **Active — flagship** | Primary revenue, most pages, most proof |
| Short-term / vacation rental property management (STR) | **Active** | Distinct nav presence, dedicated page |
| HOA management | Coming soon | Subdued tile placeholder, "Launching soon" microcopy |
| Commercial property management | Coming soon | Subdued tile placeholder, "Launching soon" microcopy |
| Homeowner Property Services | Coming soon | Subdued tile placeholder, "Launching soon" microcopy |

**Rule:** Active lines get full-color first-class cards with proof points and CTAs. Coming-soon lines get muted tiles with dashed gold rule and "Launching 2026" microcopy — visible enough to signal range, restrained enough not to dilute the proven offerings.

## The five pillars (and how each shows up)

| Pillar | Design / copy expression |
|---|---|
| **Best / unmatched** | Lead with proof, not adjectives. Years in business (since 2008), units under management, transactions, satisfaction %. Stat blocks in CowlingSans Bold. |
| **Most experienced** | Founder and team tenure. Depth of local market knowledge. "We've seen every scenario" tone. Dates and decades — never vague "experts". |
| **Highest rated** | Visible review counts and star ratings (Google, BBB, etc.) with real logos. Real testimonials in Bodoni Moda italic with gold attribution. Never stock quotes. |
| **Win/win** | Owner-first language paired with resident respect. "We treat your property like it's our own" applies to BOTH sides. Avoid landlord-vs-tenant framing. |
| **Ethical** | Transparency cues — flat fee clarity, no hidden charges, plain-English contracts, named team members with titles. No hype, no asterisks, no dark patterns. |
| **Trust your asset** | Calm, confident, low-pressure tone. Restraint = competence. No exclamation marks, no urgency timers, no popups. |

## We are a TEAM (foundational principle)

**EquityTeam is a team-first brand. The team is the product.** Real estate and property management is a craft where no single person can succeed alone — leasing, maintenance, accounting, owner relations, resident relations, legal, vendor coordination, asset oversight all happen in parallel, every day. That's only possible with a great team. The brand reflects that truth.

- **Default voice:** "we" / "our team" / "the EquityTeam team" — not "I" or any single name.
- **Default photography:** team in action, multiple people on the page, named with titles where shown.
- **When the founder appears:** Mark Thompson appears by name in the right contexts only — the founder pull quote on the homepage (already shipped), the About page founder section, and any context where a single accountable face is required (press, partnerships, a personal letter to a prospective owner). He does NOT appear as the default speaker on service pages, in case studies, or in CTAs.
- **Testimonials about the team beat testimonials about an individual.** When an owner praises a specific person (a property manager, a maintenance lead), keep it — name that team member with their title. That reinforces "great team" rather than "great founder".
- **Avoid hero-worship language.** No "Mark's vision", no "founded by a visionary", no single-person mythology. The founder built a team that built the company. Tell that story.
- **Org structure on About:** show the team as a flat grid of named, titled professionals, not a hierarchy with the founder at the top.

> Why this matters strategically: in property management specifically, an owner is asking *"who actually picks up the phone, shows up at the property, signs the lease, calls the plumber at 11pm?"* The answer is never one person — it's a team. Pretending otherwise erodes trust the moment they engage.

## Voice rules (hard)

- **Never** use "best", "leading", "premier", "top", "trusted" as bare adjectives. Replace each with a number, a date, or a named human.
  - ❌ "We are the leading property manager in Cincinnati."
  - ✅ "Managing 1,200+ doors across Greater Cincinnati since 2008."
- **Never** use exclamation marks in body copy. Authority doesn't shout. (CTA buttons may use a single trailing period or none.)
- **Default to "we" / "our team"** as the speaker. Name a specific human (with title) when the moment calls for accountability — a testimonial recipient, an authored article, a bio block — but never as the default voice of the brand.
- **Reserve founder mentions** for the homepage pull quote, the About founder section, and any press/partnership context that requires a single accountable face. Service pages, case studies, CTAs, and most marketing copy should speak as the team.
- **Always** pair a claim with a proof. "Highest rated" → star count + source. "Most experienced" → year founded + team tenure (collective).
- **Always** write to the owner first. Resident-facing copy is secondary, addressed with respect, never as adversaries.
- **No** stock testimonials, no AI-generated quotes, no anonymized "John D., Cincinnati" without verification.
- **No** urgency tactics — countdowns, "spots filling up", "limited time" — these read as cheap and erode trust.
- **No** founder hero-worship — no "Mark's vision", no single-person mythology, no org charts that put one face above the rest.

## Required proof points (collect from client before launch)

The site needs real numbers in these slots. Where unknown, use a placeholder comment in the markup, not made-up figures.

- [ ] Doors / units currently under management
- [ ] Total transactions to date (or transactions/year)
- [ ] Google review count + average star rating
- [ ] BBB rating (and accreditation date)
- [ ] Owner retention % (annual)
- [ ] Average days-on-market for placed tenants
- [ ] Eviction rate (lower = stronger trust signal)
- [ ] Year company founded → **2008** (confirmed)
- [ ] Founder bio: Mark Thompson, Founder/CEO/Broker — tenure
- [ ] Trust badges: BBB, NARPM (or other), Equal Housing, state license #

## Page-level positioning recipes

### Homepage
1. **Hero** — Bodoni italic pull quote, gold attribution. No CTA above the fold beyond the existing "Schedule a Consult".
2. **Proof bar** — 4 stat blocks (founded year, doors managed, review rating, owner retention).
3. **Service lines** — 5 tiles, 2 active full-color, 3 coming-soon muted.
4. **Featured testimonial** — Real owner quote in Bodoni italic, named + city + length-of-relationship.
5. **Trust block** — Logo row (BBB, NARPM, Google Reviews badge) above footer.

### Each service-line page
1. **Header** — H1 in CowlingSans, eyebrow in gold tracked Raleway.
2. **Positioning paragraph** — One sentence with the pillar most relevant to that service.
3. **Proof points specific to that line** — e.g. STR page: nightly average ADR managed, occupancy %, platforms supported.
4. **Process / what we do** — Numbered, plain English, no jargon.
5. **Pricing transparency block** — Flat fee disclosed or clearly explained. Ethical pillar in action.
6. **Service-specific testimonials**.
7. **CTA** — Schedule consult.

### About / Team
- Named photos with title + tenure for every team member shown.
- Founder story prominent — anchors the "most experienced" pillar.
- Values stated as commitments, not slogans.

## Anti-patterns (do not ship)

- Bare superlatives ("best", "leading", "trusted")
- Exclamation marks in headlines or body
- Stock photo testimonials
- Generic icons where real logos belong (BBB, NARPM, Google)
- Urgency timers, popups, exit-intent modals
- "Click here" / "Learn more" — CTAs should name the action ("Schedule a consult", "See LTR pricing")
- Mixed tone — folksy in one section, corporate in the next
- Coming-soon service lines styled identically to active ones (overpromise)
