Landscaping and Lawn Care: Who's Responsible, Landlord or Tenant?

June 30, 2026·4 min read

Landscaping responsibility isn't set by default law in Ohio — it's whatever your lease specifies, which makes a clear, written lawn care clause one of the most disputed-if-missing terms in a rental agreement.

Does Ohio Law Require the Landlord or Tenant to Handle Lawn Care?

Neither, by default — Ohio's landlord-tenant statute doesn't assign lawn care responsibility automatically, which means it's entirely up to the lease to specify who mows, waters, and maintains landscaping. A lease silent on this point is a common source of disputes, since both parties may reasonably assume the other is responsible.

What's the Most Common Arrangement for Single-Family Rentals?

For single-family homes, it's common to make the tenant responsible for routine lawn mowing and basic upkeep, while the landlord retains responsibility for larger landscaping elements like tree trimming, major shrub removal, or irrigation system repair. This arrangement works because it keeps day-to-day maintenance with the person actually living on the property, while capital-level landscaping decisions stay with the owner.

What's the Most Common Arrangement for Multi-Family Properties?

In duplexes, triplexes, and larger multi-family buildings, landscaping is typically the landlord's or property manager's responsibility, since shared outdoor space doesn't divide cleanly between individual tenants. This is usually handled through a landscaping vendor and factored into overall operating costs rather than assigned to any one unit.

What Should a Lawn Care Clause Actually Specify?

A clear clause should state who is responsible for mowing frequency, watering, weeding, and seasonal cleanup (leaves, snow removal if applicable), along with any expectation for tools or equipment — whether the tenant is expected to have their own mower or whether one is provided. Vague language like "tenant will maintain the yard" invites disagreement over what "maintain" actually means.

What Happens If Landscaping Is Neglected?

If a tenant is contractually responsible for lawn care and neglects it, this is typically treated as a lease violation and can factor into deposit deductions or, in repeated cases, lease non-renewal. If the landlord is responsible and neglects it, a tenant may have grounds to raise a habitability or lease-compliance concern, particularly if neglect creates a genuine safety or pest issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I require a tenant to hire a lawn service instead of mowing themselves?
Yes, if specified in the lease — some landlords require tenants to maintain proof of a lawn service to ensure consistent upkeep, though this should be clearly stated as a lease term rather than assumed.

Is snow removal typically the landlord's or tenant's responsibility?
This varies by lease and property type — for single-family homes it's often assigned to the tenant for their own driveway and walkway, while multi-family properties usually handle it through the landlord or property manager for shared areas.

What if my lease doesn't mention lawn care at all?
Reach out to your landlord or property manager to clarify before a dispute arises — silence in the lease doesn't default to either party under Ohio law, so it's worth getting a clear answer in writing.
