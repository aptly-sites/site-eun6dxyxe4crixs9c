Renting to Section 8 Tenants in Cincinnati: What Landlords Need to Know

June 30, 2026·4 min read

Renting to a tenant with a Housing Choice Voucher works differently than a standard lease, and within Cincinnati city limits, it's also a legal requirement landlords need to understand. Here's what actually changes when a Section 8 voucher is involved.

What Is the Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher Program?

The Housing Choice Voucher program (commonly called Section 8) is a federally funded rental assistance program administered locally by a Public Housing Authority, which pays a portion of a qualified tenant's rent directly to the landlord each month. The tenant pays the remaining portion, typically capped around 30% of their household income, directly to the landlord as well.

Is It Legal to Refuse a Section 8 Applicant in Cincinnati?

No — within Cincinnati city limits, refusing to rent to an otherwise-qualified applicant solely because they intend to pay with a housing voucher is illegal under the city's local Fair Housing ordinance, which protects source of income. Landlords can still apply the same income, credit, and background screening criteria they'd apply to any applicant — the voucher itself simply cannot be the disqualifying factor.

How Does Rent Get Paid With a Housing Choice Voucher?

Once a tenant is approved, the landlord signs a Housing Assistance Payments (HAP) contract with the local Public Housing Authority, which specifies the rent amount and the split between the voucher payment and the tenant's portion. The Public Housing Authority then pays its portion directly to the landlord by the agreed date each month, independent of whether the tenant's portion is paid — though a persistent failure by the tenant to pay their share is still handled through the normal lease enforcement process.

What Is a Housing Quality Standards Inspection?

Before a voucher tenant can move in, the Public Housing Authority conducts a Housing Quality Standards (HQS) inspection to confirm the property meets basic safety and habitability requirements — functioning smoke detectors, safe electrical systems, adequate heat, and no significant structural hazards. This inspection typically happens annually thereafter as well, in addition to any inspections the landlord conducts independently.

Can I Still Screen Section 8 Applicants the Same Way?

Yes — landlords can and should apply the same credit, income, rental history, and background screening criteria to voucher holders as to any other applicant, as long as the criteria is applied consistently across all applicants regardless of payment source. The one adjustment: income verification for a voucher holder should account for the fact that a portion of their rent-paying ability comes from the voucher itself, not solely their personal income.

What Are the Benefits of Renting to Voucher Holders?

A meaningful portion of the rent arrives as a reliable, government-backed payment each month, which reduces exposure to a tenant's personal income volatility. Voucher holders also tend to stay longer in a given unit than the broader rental market average, since losing a voucher-approved unit means restarting a sometimes lengthy search and re-inspection process — which can translate into lower turnover costs for the landlord.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to accept Section 8 tenants in Cincinnati?
Within Cincinnati city limits, yes — refusing an otherwise-qualified applicant because they intend to pay with a housing voucher violates the city's source-of-income protection, though landlords may still apply consistent screening criteria to all applicants.

How much of the rent does the Housing Authority actually pay?
It varies by household, but the Public Housing Authority typically covers the portion of rent above what the tenant can afford at roughly 30% of their income, with the tenant paying the remainder directly to the landlord.

What happens if my property fails the Housing Quality Standards inspection?
The Public Housing Authority will identify the specific issues that need correction, and the voucher payment won't begin (or will be paused for existing tenancies) until the property passes a follow-up inspection.
