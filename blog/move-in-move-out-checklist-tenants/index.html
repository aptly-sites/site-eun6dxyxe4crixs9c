Move-In and Move-Out Checklist for Cincinnati Tenants

January 20, 2026·4 min read

Protecting your security deposit and starting a tenancy off on the right foot both come down to the same habit: documentation. Here's what to do at move-in and move-out to protect yourself at every stage of your lease.

What should a tenant document before move-in day?

Before you unpack a single box, walk through the property with your phone and photograph or video every room — including closets, cabinets, appliances, floors, walls, and any existing damage or wear, no matter how minor. Date-stamp these photos and store them somewhere reliable (cloud storage, email to yourself) where you'll be able to find them a year or more from now. These photos are your primary evidence if a deposit dispute ever arises.

What should a tenant check during the move-in walkthrough?

If your landlord or property manager offers a move-in inspection, take it seriously and don't rush through it:

  • Test every light switch, outlet, and appliance
  • Run water at every faucet and check for drainage issues
  • Open and close every window and door
  • Check smoke and carbon monoxide detectors and note their locations
  • Note any existing scuffs, stains, or damage in writing on the inspection form
  • Confirm you and your landlord both sign and receive a copy of the completed report

If no formal inspection is offered, request one — or at minimum, send your landlord a written summary of the property's condition with your photos within the first few days.

What utility and address changes should I handle at move-in?

Confirm which utilities you're responsible for per your lease, and set up service before or immediately at move-in so you're never without power, water, or heat. Update your address with the post office, your bank, your employer, and any subscriptions — a simple step that's easy to forget in the chaos of moving but that creates real problems if neglected.

How should a tenant keep records during a tenancy?

Every maintenance request, every conversation about a lease term, and every payment should leave a written paper trail — submit requests in writing (email or a tenant portal, not just a phone call) and keep a simple folder of your lease, receipts, and any correspondence with your landlord. You likely won't need most of it, but if you ever do, you'll be glad it's organized and accessible.

How should a tenant prepare for move-out?

Review your lease for the required notice period — often 30 or 60 days — and give written notice by that deadline. Schedule your move with enough time to thoroughly clean the property afterward, since a rushed final cleaning is one of the most common reasons Cincinnati tenants lose part of their security deposit.

What should a tenant do during the move-out walkthrough?

Request a move-out inspection and walk through the property yourself first, comparing it against your move-in photos. Clean thoroughly — including appliances, baseboards, and inside cabinets — and photograph the empty property room by room before you hand over keys. If your landlord identifies any damage during the walkthrough, ask for it to be documented in writing on the spot.

How does a Cincinnati tenant get their security deposit back?

In Ohio, your landlord has 30 days from the date you vacate to return your deposit along with an itemized list of any deductions. Provide your forwarding address in writing at move-out so that clock starts running immediately. If you haven't received anything after 30 days, follow up in writing — and know that Ohio law provides a double-damages remedy if a landlord wrongfully withholds a deposit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is "normal wear and tear" in a Cincinnati rental and why does it matter?
Normal wear and tear is the ordinary deterioration from everyday use — minor wall scuffs, small nail holes, carpet wear in traffic areas. These cannot be deducted from your security deposit under Ohio law. Damage beyond that — stains, large holes, broken fixtures from misuse — can be deducted. Documenting the property's condition at move-in is your best protection against having normal wear and tear incorrectly classified as damage.

What if my landlord doesn't return my security deposit within 30 days?
Under Ohio Revised Code 5321.16, if your landlord fails to return the deposit and itemized deduction list within 30 days, you may be entitled to the full deposit back plus an additional amount equal to what was wrongfully withheld, plus attorney's fees. Document your forwarding address delivery and any follow-up communications carefully.

Should I do a professional cleaning before move-out from a Cincinnati rental?
A professional cleaning is not legally required but is often worth the cost — typically $150–$300 for a standard single-family home. It ensures the unit meets the "broom clean" standard most leases require, reduces the risk of cleaning charges being deducted from your deposit, and leaves the relationship with your landlord on a positive note for references.
