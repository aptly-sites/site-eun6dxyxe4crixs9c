Quick Guide to Effective Tenant Screening in Cincinnati, OH

March 7, 2024·4 min read

Tenant screening is the most important thing you'll do as a landlord. The right tenant pays on time, takes care of your property, and stays for multiple lease terms. The wrong one can cost you thousands in unpaid rent, property damage, and legal fees. A rigorous, consistent screening process protects your investment.

What should a rental application include?

Require every adult applicant (18+) to complete a rental application that captures full legal name, date of birth, Social Security Number, current and previous addresses with landlord contact information, employment history, monthly income and sources, bankruptcy or eviction history, vehicle information, and pet information. Incomplete applications should be returned for completion or declined — serious applicants complete them fully.

What does a credit check reveal about a rental applicant?

Credit history reveals patterns of financial behavior — not just a number. Look beyond the score and examine payment history, collections (especially utility or rent-related), debt-to-income indicators, and public records like judgments or bankruptcies. A score of 620+ is a common threshold, but context matters — a 580 with clean rental history and strong income can be more reliable than a 650 with rent-related collections.

How much income should a tenant earn to qualify for a rental?

The standard benchmark is that tenants should earn at least 3x the monthly rent in gross income — verify this with pay stubs, offer letters, tax returns for self-employed applicants, or bank statements. Never accept income claims without documentation; this is one of the most commonly falsified elements of a rental application.

What questions should I ask a previous landlord during screening?

Contact previous landlords — not just the current one, who may be motivated to provide a positive reference to move out a problem tenant — and ask specifically: Did they pay rent on time? Was the unit left in good condition? Were there any lease violations? Would you rent to them again? These direct questions cut through vague references.

How should landlords handle criminal background checks for rental applicants?

A criminal background check should be reviewed carefully and evaluated in context — not used as an automatic disqualifier. Focus on violent crimes, property crimes, and drug manufacturing charges that could represent a risk to neighbors or the property. Follow Fair Housing guidelines and apply consistent criteria to all applicants to avoid discrimination claims.

How do I check if a rental applicant has prior evictions?

Check national eviction databases as part of your standard process — in Ohio, eviction records are public court records searchable through the Ohio Supreme Court's online docket. A prior eviction is a significant red flag; it means another landlord reached the point of legal action, which is the last resort for most landlords.

Why is applying screening criteria consistently so important?

Applying the same criteria to every applicant is both a Fair Housing requirement and your legal protection — document your screening criteria in writing and keep records of all applications and screening decisions. Inconsistent application of standards, even unintentionally, creates Fair Housing exposure and makes it harder to defend your decisions if challenged.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Cincinnati landlord reject an applicant based on credit score alone?
Yes, if you apply the same credit threshold to every applicant and it is stated in your written screening criteria. However, a blanket rejection based solely on score without reviewing the full credit report context is generally considered poor practice and may miss strong applicants. Always review the full picture.

What is application fraud and how common is it?
Application fraud — falsifying income, employment, or prior rental history on a rental application — is increasingly common and is one of the leading predictors of future eviction. Verify every material claim with documentation; never rely solely on what an applicant self-reports.

How does Fair Housing law affect tenant screening in Cincinnati?
Fair Housing law prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, or disability. Cincinnati also extends protections to source of income. Apply written, documented criteria consistently to every applicant — this is your strongest protection against a Fair Housing complaint.
