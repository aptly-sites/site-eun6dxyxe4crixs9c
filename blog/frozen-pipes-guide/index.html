A Tenant's Guide to Frozen Pipes

January 14, 2021·4 min read

Cincinnati winters regularly bring temperatures below freezing — and when they do, frozen pipes are one of the most common and costly problems that rental property tenants and landlords face. A frozen pipe can burst and flood a property in minutes. Here's what every renter needs to know.

Which pipes are most likely to freeze in a Cincinnati rental?

The most vulnerable pipes are those in exterior walls (especially north-facing walls), under sinks on exterior walls, in unheated spaces like garages, crawl spaces, attics, and basements, outdoor hose bibs and irrigation lines, and pipes in poorly insulated areas near exterior doors or windows. Knowing which pipes are at risk lets you focus your prevention efforts where they matter most.

How can a renter prevent frozen pipes during a Cincinnati winter?

Preventing frozen pipes is largely within a tenant's control: keep the thermostat above 55°F at all times even when away, open cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks on exterior walls to let warm air reach the pipes, let faucets drip on very cold nights (below 20°F) to keep water moving, disconnect garden hoses from exterior spigots before the first freeze, and know where the main water shutoff valve is before you need it.

What should I do if a pipe freezes in my rental?

If you turn on a faucet and get only a trickle or nothing at all, you likely have a frozen pipe: contact your landlord immediately, turn off the water at the main shutoff, never use an open flame to thaw a pipe (this is a serious fire hazard), and use safe thawing methods like a hair dryer, heating pad, or warm towels applied to the pipe. Open the faucet connected to the frozen section before applying heat so steam can escape.

What should I do if a pipe bursts in my Cincinnati rental?

A burst pipe is a true emergency: shut off the main water supply immediately, turn off electricity at the breaker if water is near any electrical outlets or fixtures, call your landlord or property manager immediately, document the damage with photos before cleanup, and begin removing standing water and affected items to limit secondary damage and potential mold.

Who is responsible for frozen or burst pipes — the landlord or the tenant?

Landlords are responsible for maintaining the property in habitable condition, which includes adequate insulation and functional plumbing. If the property has known vulnerabilities — uninsulated pipes, poor weatherstripping — bring them to your landlord's attention in writing before winter. However, if a pipe freezes because a tenant turned off the heat while away, the tenant may bear responsibility for resulting damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what temperature do pipes freeze in a Cincinnati home?
Pipes can begin to freeze when temperatures drop to around 20°F (-6°C) or below, particularly if they're in unheated or poorly insulated areas. Interior pipes in heated spaces are rarely at risk above 0°F, but exterior walls and unheated crawl spaces can reach freezing temperatures even when outside temperatures are in the 20s.

Should I notify my landlord before leaving a Cincinnati rental for an extended winter vacation?
Yes — notify your landlord before any extended absence during winter and ensure the heat is set to at least 60°F. Many Ohio leases include provisions requiring tenants to keep heat at minimum temperatures and notify the landlord of extended absences. Failure to do so can make the tenant liable for freeze-related damage.

Does renters insurance cover burst pipe damage to a tenant's belongings?
Yes — standard renters insurance covers personal property damage from sudden, accidental water damage including burst pipes. It also covers additional living expenses if the unit becomes temporarily uninhabitable. Gradual leaks or damage from a tenant's own negligence may be excluded; review your policy's specific terms.
